7. How to analyse your real data

1️⃣

Count responses

For each pre-test and post-test question, count how many students answered correctly. Convert each count to a percentage of students who took the test, so the two tests can be compared even if a few students were absent for one of them.

2️⃣

Compare concept by concept

Put the pre-test and post-test figures side by side for the same concept. The change between them is the finding. Report every concept, including any where the change was small or negative.

3️⃣

Look at the free-text answers

The most useful comments are usually in the open-ended questions. Group similar answers together and quote one or two of them — but never in a way that identifies a particular student.

Ethics reminder for the real data

Students are minors. Match pre-test and post-test papers using an anonymous code (for example a number rather than a name), keep the responses secure, do not publish any photograph without written permission, and never report a result that could identify an individual student. If permission for photographs is refused, use diagrams and the attendance record instead — that is a perfectly acceptable form of evidence.
